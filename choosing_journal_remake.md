## Why use this page?

Use this page to make a well-motivated journal choice for your manuscript. It helps you identify suitable journals, compare alternatives, evaluate publication conditions, and avoid questionable publishers.

It can help you:

- Find the best journal for your topic, subject area, and audience.
- Compare several journals in your field.
- Identify backup journals in case your manuscript is rejected.
- Discover relevant journals you may not have considered before.
- Check submission-to-decision time, publication time, and acceptance rate where available.
- Avoid questionable publishers, sometimes called predatory publishers, for example with help from the [KIB video](https://kib.ki.se/en/publish-analyse/strategic-publishing/questionable-journals).

A good outcome is a clear and defensible decision, for example: “This journal fits my topic, article type, audience, indexing needs, open access requirements, and practical conditions.”

When you submit, this preparation also makes it easier to explain in your cover letter why the manuscript fits the journal’s scope.

## 0. Start with local publishing practice

Begin by looking at the publishing tradition in your department or research group. There is often a good reason why colleagues return to the same journals: those journals may be central venues for the field, read by the right audience, and familiar to supervisors or collaborators.

This can be thought of as publishing in the “comfort zone.” It is often a sensible starting point, but not the only option.

## 1. Find candidate journals

The goal here is to enter an abstract, and sometimes also a title and keywords, into a journal-matching service to find journals that have published similar work before.

### Important first step

**Read the journal’s aims and scope, and read the instructions for authors.**

### General, non-publisher-specific tools

- [Web of Science Master Journal List Manuscript Matcher](https://mjl.clarivate.com/home) — login required (free); click **Match Manuscript** and sign in.
- [JANE](http://jane.biosemantics.org/) — biomedicine only; paste in your abstract and search.
- [JOT](https://jot.publichealth.yale.edu/search) — builds on JANE results and adds similarity statistics and journal impact information; developed by the Yale School of Public Health.
- [B!SON](https://service.tib.eu/bison/) — helps identify suitable open access journals using semantic and bibliometric methods.

### Publisher-specific tools

- [Elsevier JournalFinder](https://journalfinder.elsevier.com/)
- [IEEE Publication Recommender](https://publication-recommender.ieee.org/home)
- [Springer Nature Journal Suggester](https://journalsuggester.springer.com/)
- [Taylor & Francis Journal Suggester](https://authstaging.wpengine.com/publishing-your-research/choosing-a-journal/journal-suggester/)
- [Wiley JournalFinder](https://journalfinder.wiley.com/search?type=match)

### Cascaded peer review

Cascaded peer review is a process where a manuscript rejected by one journal, together with its referee reports, is transferred directly to another journal, usually within the same publisher, instead of starting peer review again from the beginning.

## 2. Check coverage in bibliographic databases

The aim is to check whether a journal is indexed in the major databases used in your field. Good indexing improves findability and visibility, and helps ensure that colleagues can discover your article through the sources they already use.

This is often called **database indexing**.

> Google Scholar is not a major bibliographic database, even though it is widely used for searching.
>
> Database coverage is often listed on journal websites; see this [Taylor & Francis example](https://www.tandfonline.com/action/journalInformation?journalCode=cdss20).

### General tools

- [MIAR](https://miar.ub.edu/) — comprehensive database with coverage information for 40,000+ journals; from Universitat de Barcelona.
- [Web of Science Master Journal List](https://mjl.clarivate.com/home) — also referred to here as Science Citation Index; journal profile pages are visible after login.
- [Scopus Sources](https://www.scopus.com/sources.uri?zone=TopNavBar&origin=sbrowse)

### Subject-specific tools

- [Analytical Abstracts](http://pubs.rsc.org/lus/analytical-abstracts/about#source-list)
- [Art & Architecture Complete](https://www.ebscohost.com/titleLists/vth-coverage.htm)
- [Chemical Abstracts / SciFinder](https://cassi.cas.org/search.jsp)
- [EconLit](https://www.aeaweb.org/econlit/journal_list.php)
- [Ei Compendex](https://www.elsevier.com/solutions/engineering-village/content/compendex) — find the Excel sheet on the page.
- [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?journals)
- [GEOBASE](https://www.elsevier.com/solutions/engineering-village/content/geobase) — find the Excel sheet on the page.
- [GeoRef](https://www.americangeosciences.org/information/georef/serials)
- [Inspec](https://www.theiet.org/publishing/inspec/inspec-content-coverage)
- [Library, Information Science & Technology Abstracts](https://www.ebscohost.com/titleLists/lih-coverage.htm)
- [MEDLINE / PubMed](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nlmcatalog/)
- [Materials Science & Engineering Database](http://tls.search.proquest.com/titlelist/jsp/list/tlsSingle.jsp?productId=10000250&_ga=2.38453600.2129395531.1599734075-1514362537.1599734075)
- [MathSciNet](https://mathscinet.ams.org/) — click the **Journals** tab.
- [zbMATH](https://zbmath.org/serials/)

## 3a. Check journal ranking in the subject area

The aim is to compare journals using citation-based indicators. In everyday language, this is often reduced to “impact factor,” but several indicators are available and they measure different things.

> Never compare journals from different subject areas unless the indicator is field-normalized.
>
> JCI, SJR, and SNIP are field-normalized, while JIF and CiteScore are not.

- [CWTS Journal Indicators](https://www.journalindicators.com/) — indicator: [SNIP](https://www.journalindicators.com/methodology); based on citation data from Scopus and Web of Science.
- [Journal Citation Reports](https://jcr.clarivate.com/) — indicators: [Journal Impact Factor (JIF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_factor) and [Journal Citation Indicator (JCI)](https://clarivate.com/blog/introducing-the-journal-citation-indicator-a-new-field-normalized-measurement-of-journal-citation-impact/); based on citation data from Web of Science.
- [SCImago Journal & Country Rank](https://www.scimagojr.com/) — indicator: [SJR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCImago_Journal_Rank); based on citation data from Scopus.
- [Scopus Sources](https://www.scopus.com/sources) — indicators: [CiteScore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CiteScore), [SJR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCImago_Journal_Rank), and [SNIP](https://www.journalindicators.com/methodology); based on citation data from Scopus.
- [Norwegian list](https://kanalregister.hkdir.no/publiseringskanaler/Forside.action?request_locale=en) — indicators: 2, 1, 0, or X; 2 is the highest level and X indicates questionable journals; based on expert panels.

## 3b. Check conference ranking in the subject area

The aim is to compare conferences using available ranking or impact measures.

> Do not compare conferences across different subject areas.

- [Scopus Sources](https://www.scopus.com/sources) — choose **Source Type = Conference Proceedings** in the left-hand menu; indicators include CiteScore, SJR, and SNIP.
- [Conference Rank](http://www.conferenceranks.com/) — an older ranking that may still be useful; indicators include ERA 2010, Qualis 2012, and Microsoft Academic Graph 2014.

## 4. Check time to decision, acceptance rate, and review information

Practical publishing conditions matter. Try to find information about time to first decision, total publication time, acceptance rate, and the review model used by the journal.

Useful places to check include:

- The journal website.
- Publisher information pages.
- Community sources such as [SciRev](https://scirev.org/).

Use these figures carefully, since they may be incomplete, selectively reported, or field-dependent.

---

## 5. Check open access options and APCs

Check whether the journal is subscription-based, hybrid, or fully open access, and whether article processing charges (APCs) apply. Also check whether your institution or library has agreements that cover APCs.

Relevant tools and pages include:

- The journal website.
- Publisher open access information pages.
- [Open policy finder / Sherpa Romeo](https://v2.sherpa.ac.uk/romeo/).
- Local library guidance and publishing agreements.

---

## 6. Check for warning signs

Before submitting, make sure the journal is legitimate and suitable. Look at the journal website, editorial board, indexing claims, peer review information, contact details, and publisher reputation.

Helpful resources include:

- The [KIB video on questionable journals](https://kib.ki.se/en/publish-analyse/strategic-publishing/questionable-journals).
- The [Norwegian list](https://kanalregister.hkdir.no/publiseringskanaler/Forside.action?request_locale=en), where X indicates questionable journals.

## Working method

A practical workflow is:

1. Start with journals already used in your research environment.
2. Use matching tools to build a list of candidate journals.
3. Read aims and scope, and author instructions, for each serious candidate.
4. Check indexing in the databases that matter in your field.
5. Compare ranking indicators within the same subject area.
6. Review practical conditions such as turnaround time, APCs, and peer review model.
7. Exclude questionable or unsuitable journals.
8. Choose one primary target journal and one or two backup options.

---

## Final note

The goal is not to find the “best” journal in the abstract. The goal is to find the most suitable journal for **your** manuscript, audience, field, and publishing constraints.
